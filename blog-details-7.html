Learning Reflections: Leading Conversation on Responsible Media Reporting on Suicide and Mental Health 🧠

Recent 2 min read Sagar Parajuli
Disclaimer: Skip this if you are sensitive to the topic.
Program Banner: Responsible Reporting on Suicide and Mental Health — Media Professionals and Journalist Orientation Program

Yesterday, I had the privilege of leading a session for journalists and media professionals on responsible reporting of suicide. The experience was unlike any other program I’ve led… it was intense, deeply unsettling at moments, and left me with a profound sense of responsibility.

At the start, I went through a rigorous review of media reports on suicide cases. Some were very disturbing, and I had to hold myself together while trying to understand them from the perspective of vulnerable individuals to the best I can.

Then I reviewed global and local guidelines, including Preventing Suicide: Resource for Media Professionals (2023 Update) and the Government of Nepal’s Guideline for Reporting Suicide for Media Professionals. While looking at guidelines, I initially focused on being “sensible,” but later learning from there, I realized that responsive reporting grounded in care and responsibility is far more important than simply being cautious.

Understanding Global Media Effects

Further, I also explored two different phenomena: the Werther effect—how irresponsible reporting can unintentionally increase suicide risk, and the Papageno effect, which shows that responsible, hope-focused reporting can prevent suicides and promote resilience. Understanding these contrasting effects made it even clearer how critical media responsibility is in shaping real-world outcomes. And this was more technical and based on facts from all around the world.

Werther Effect

Named after the German writer Goethe’s novel ‘The Sorrows of Young Werther’, after which many young people imitated and died by suicide, emphasizing the risk of irresponsible reporting.

Papageno Effect

Named after a character called Papageno in Mozart’s opera ‘The Magic Flute’ who considered suicide initially and refrained later when presented with coping strategies, illustrating how responsible and hope-focused reporting can prevent suicide.

Personal Reflections

In reflection, the 45-minute session I delivered standing in front was far easier than holding the weight of these stories and data behind the slides. It reminded me how essential it is to have honest dialogue and how careful we must be when crafting and releasing news about suicide.

So, I humbly request every journalist, media professional, content creator, and social media user alike to practice responsive thinking before sharing anything about suicide.

Words matter. Framing matters. Lives can depend on it.
