The Story of Two Lost Lives and Many Missed Opportunities in Between to Prevent: A Maternal Death in Nawalparasi East

Apr 8, 2026 4 min read Sagar Parajuli

Maternal deaths often carry stories that statistics alone cannot convey. Behind every number is a life, a family, and a system struggling to see the unseen. Recently, a young woman in Nawalparasi (East of Bardaghat Susta) lost her life during childbirth due to rheumatic heart disease (RHD), a condition that remained undisclosed during her antenatal visits and was only revealed through a verbal autopsy after her death. What makes her story especially poignant is that she was exceptionally health-conscious, consulting multiple hospitals and laboratories every month, following medical advice closely, and surrounded by a family deeply committed to her well-being. Yet, despite all these measures, a silent risk had gone unnoticed, demonstrating how even proactive care can be undermined when underlying vulnerabilities remain hidden.

Her life illustrates a paradox often overlooked in maternal health: vigilance and hope do not always guarantee safety. After being diagnosed with RHD, she had taken care of her health very carefully, attending check-ups, running tests, and seeking second opinions whenever necessary. After her first child, a doctor had explicitly advised her not to conceive again due to her heart condition. Yet, motivated by the dream of giving her first child a sibling, she quietly chose to try again, keeping her history undisclosed. In doing so, she unknowingly placed herself at the intersection of human aspiration and systemic gaps, a space where even minor oversights can become fatal… and the story of two lost lives happened.

Beneath the Surface: The Iceberg of Risk

On the outside, she appeared perfectly healthy and proactive. To her healthcare providers, she was diligent, compliant, and motivated. But like the tip of an iceberg, what was visible—her regular clinic visits, lab results, and check-ups—was only a fraction of the real risk. Her RHD, the silent condition lurking below, remained invisible because it was never disclosed. The system saw her adherence but missed the hidden warnings from her past consultations and prior medical advice. This invisible layer of risk highlights a critical lesson: in maternal health, what is not seen can be as dangerous as what is visible.

Hope and Determination: The Mother’s Motivation or the Risk

Her desire to give birth again was deeply human and motivated by love for her first child and hope for a growing family; she consciously chose not to share her heart condition with her ANC providers. Her story reveals a universal truth in maternal care: personal aspirations can influence disclosure, and without sensitive history-taking, even the most proactive patient can unknowingly mask serious risks. Her hope, determination, and optimism, while admirable, became a tragic factor in a context where system-level vigilance was insufficient.

The Silent Threat of RHD

Rheumatic heart disease is often a slow, silent process, following untreated infections in childhood and gradually damaging heart valves. During pregnancy, increased blood volume and cardiac strain can push a previously manageable condition into life-threatening territory. Even women who appear healthy, attend clinics, and seek expert advice can face sudden complications if RHD goes unrecognized. In her case, routine lab visits and consultations could not compensate for the absence of a structured system to detect hidden cardiac risks.

Cracks in the System: Gaps on Both Sides

While the concealment of her condition contributed to the tragedy, systemic gaps also played a crucial role. Antenatal care protocols did not prompt providers to ask specifically about previous high-risk medical advice, cardiac history, or prior counseling against conception. Providers lacked tools and procedures to uncover concealed conditions gently and empathetically, and multidisciplinary follow-up for high-risk patients was absent. The system failed to anticipate that patient motivation, hope, family planning desires, or fear can influence disclosure, leaving critical information invisible and unaddressed.

Lessons for the Future

Her death underscores the urgent need to rethink antenatal care, moving beyond what is visible to detect risks that lie beneath the surface.

  • Structured Cardiac Screening: Integrating routine cardiac evaluation checkpoints within standard ANC protocols.
  • Sensitive History-Taking: Training healthcare providers with empathetic communication tools to surface past high-risk medical advice.
  • Multidisciplinary Follow-Up: Building care pathways that connect high-risk maternal patients with specialized medical supervision.
  • Community & Patient Education: Empowering mothers to understand the critical importance of disclosure, even when personal hopes or fears make it difficult.
"Every maternal death is a warning, and hers reminds us that vigilance, hope, and support are not enough if the hidden risks remain invisible. Prevention requires seeing the full iceberg, not just the part above the waterline, and ensuring that every mother, no matter how health-conscious, receives care that truly accounts for both her body and her story."

This case is a painful reminder that even the most health-conscious women can be lost when critical risks remain hidden. It reinforces my belief that maternal care must go beyond routine checklists to truly understand each woman’s story and unseen vulnerabilities.
